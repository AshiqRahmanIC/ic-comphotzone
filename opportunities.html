Opportunities

Do computational photonics research as an undergraduate.

ComPhotZone projects are designed so students can enter through physics, coding, or both — then grow toward independent numerical research and scientific communication.

Who fits here?

Curiosity matters more than prior photonics experience.

Students interested in physics, engineering, mathematics, computer science, or scientific computing can contribute. Many projects can begin with a manageable numerical problem and grow into a research poster, summer project, or manuscript contribution.

  • Interested in waves, optics, electromagnetism, or scientific computing
  • Willing to learn Python and numerical methods
  • Comfortable asking questions and debugging models
  • Interested in presenting research clearly

Typical research workflow

  1. Read the core physics and identify a research question.
  2. Reproduce or build a numerical model.
  3. Validate against theory or published results.
  4. Run parameter studies and analyze trends.
  5. Create publication-quality figures and interpret the physics.
  6. Present the work as a poster, talk, report, or manuscript.
Possible student directions

Projects in the zone

🌈

Supercontinuum design maps

Explore how wavelength, peak power, dispersion, or geometry changes the generated optical spectrum.

🧭

Dispersion engineering

Calculate guided modes, effective index, GVD, zero-dispersion wavelengths, and phase-matching behavior.

⏱

Temporal photonics

Reproduce temporal-mirror or time-lens simulations and test how the dynamics respond to model parameters.

〰️

Build an FDTD solver

Model interference, diffraction, resonances, or scattering and validate the solver against analytical cases.

📈

Scientific visualization

Develop analysis and plotting workflows for spectra, spectrograms, mode fields, and propagation maps.

💻

Open photonics tools

Build reproducible workflows using Python, MPB/Meep, gdsfactory, and version-controlled research code.

⚡

Hybrid mode-locked lasers

Model pulse-train formation and investigate how laser parameters influence ultrashort-pulse stability and temporal structure.

🔐

All-optical cryptography

Explore nonlinear optical signal processing using quantum-dot semiconductor optical amplifiers and photonic logic concepts.
